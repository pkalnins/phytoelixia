About This Project

The Salutogenic Medicine Project is a working clinical and teaching reference built around a single thesis: modern medicine has a well-developed answer to pathogenesis (what causes disease, and how to eliminate it) but no comparable foundation for salutogenesis (what generates and sustains health). This project treats salutogenic medicine not as a replacement for biomedicine but as its missing half, and attempts to give it a real foundation rather than just a friendlier vocabulary.

That foundation draws on three sources, integrated rather than merely translated between one another: Traditional Chinese Medicine's Yang/Qi/Blood/Yin pattern differentiation as the clinical vocabulary; Rudolf Steiner's anthroposophic physiology — the fourfold human being, the four ethers, the twelve organ processes — as the ontological account of what that capacity actually is; and modern physiology's own independent case for it, from homeostasis and allostasis through psychoneuroimmunology to network physiology and cellular bioelectricity. Western herbalism supplies much of the clinical material at the point of treatment. The Foundations section's opening chapters lay out why real integration has to go down to each tradition's own ontology and epistemology, rather than stopping at shared vocabulary.

How it's organized. Foundations lays the shared philosophical and physiological groundwork once, read ahead of everything else. Glands and Organs each build out that framework for a specific part of the body — phenomenology, physiology, clinical patterns, and the biomedical conditions those patterns predominate in — and Framework holds the eight universal pattern types (Yang/Qi/Blood/Yin × Excess/Deficiency) that every organ and gland chapter is built from.

Where it stands. This is a living, actively-drafted document, not a finished text. The Lung module is the most complete, built from real clinical source material; the other organ modules are first-pass drafts pending clinical review, and several Foundations chapters and all seven glands are still placeholders. Treat everything here as a draft mentorship-group edition — a working reference for an active teaching group, not a publication.
